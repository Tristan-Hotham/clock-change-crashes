## Suitable journals (no fee to publish on the standard route; checked in 2026)

1. **Accident Analysis and Prevention** (Elsevier). A hybrid journal: publishing on the subscription route has no fee; open access is optional and paid. It has published most of the work this paper engages with (Sullivan and Flannagan 2002; Coate and Markowitz 2004; Johansson, Wanvik and Elvik 2009; Uttley and Fotios 2017; Robb and Barnes 2018; Lei et al. 2026). I could not confirm a fixed word limit for research papers from the journal's public pages; check the current Guide for Authors before submitting. The paper's text before the references is about 8,800 words including tables, which is long for the journal's typical article, so expect to move the robustness table and the district analysis to supplementary material.
2. **Journal of Safety Research** (Elsevier, with the National Safety Council). Also hybrid, with no fee on the subscription route. It published Huang and Levinson (2010) and Woods, Weast and Monfort (2025) on the same question. Its word limit was not confirmed; check the Guide for Authors.

Considered and not recommended as a first choice:

## Summary of the contribution (150 words)

Does changing the clocks cause road crashes? Using every personal-injury collision reported to the police in Great Britain from 1979 to 2025 (9.1 million) and an analysis plan written before the outcome was examined, I compare the week after each of 91 clock changes with the same weekdays three weeks either side. Collisions changed by 0.8% after the spring change (95% CI -1.5 to 3.1) and by -0.1% after the autumn change (-2.3 to 2.1). Fatal collisions after the autumn change were 4.2% higher (-0.3 to 8.9), an estimate matched by 2 of 29 placebo Sundays. Within each day, the autumn change moved evening-peak collisions from 68% to 88% dark, yet raised their share by only 3.9%, and the spring change did not show the mirror image. The paper offers the longest single-design British evidence, separates whole-day from within-day effects, and benchmarks every estimate against ordinary weeks.
